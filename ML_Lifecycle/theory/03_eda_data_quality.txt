STAGE 3: DATA CLEANING AND EXPLORATORY DATA ANALYSIS
================================================================================

Scope note: mechanics of imputation, outlier treatment, encoding and scaling
live in ../../FeatureEngineering/theory/. This file covers what EDA is FOR, the
quality checks that belong in a pipeline rather than a notebook, and the
diagnostics (distributions, correlation, multicollinearity, leakage) that decide
what you do next.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT EDA IS ACTUALLY FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

EDA is not "make plots". It answers five questions:

    1. Is this dataset what the documentation claims it is? (schema, grain,
       row counts, date ranges)
    2. What is the GRAIN of a row - one row per what? Most join bugs are grain
       bugs. Verify with a uniqueness check on the claimed key.
    3. What is broken, missing or impossible, and is the brokenness random or
       structured?
    4. What is the shape of the target, and which features plausibly relate
       to it?
    5. Is anything too good to be true? (leakage detection)

Do all of it on the TRAINING SPLIT only. Looking at the test set - even just
plotting it - leaks information through your own decisions. If you must sanity-
check the test set, check schema and row counts, not distributions or
relationships to the target.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE DATA QUALITY CHECKLIST (AUTOMATE THIS, DO NOT EYEBALL IT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Per dataset:
    - row count vs expectation, and per-day row count over time (a step change
      is an upstream incident)
    - primary key uniqueness; grain matches the documented grain
    - referential integrity on join keys (orphan rate)
    - date range, timezone consistency, future-dated rows

Per column:
    - dtype and dtype stability across partitions
    - null rate, and null rate over time
    - cardinality (a categorical that jumps from 30 to 30,000 levels is a bug)
    - range/domain violations (negative age, latitude > 90, price = 0)
    - sentinel values masquerading as data: -1, 999, 0, "N/A", "", "unknown",
      1970-01-01, 9999-12-31. These must be converted to NaN BEFORE any
      statistics are computed, or every mean you look at is wrong.
    - unit consistency (cents vs dollars, metres vs feet, ms vs s) - usually
      revealed by a bimodal histogram three orders of magnitude apart
    - encoding/whitespace/case chaos in strings ("US", "us", " US ")

Write these as assertions in the pipeline (Great Expectations, pandera, or plain
asserts). Checks that live only in a notebook protect nothing. Each check needs
a severity: fail the run vs warn and continue.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. MISSING DATA: THE PART THAT IS ANALYSIS, NOT MECHANICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Classify the mechanism before choosing a fix:
    MCAR  missingness independent of everything. Rare. Dropping rows is safe
          but wasteful.
    MAR   missingness depends on OBSERVED features (income missing more often
          for younger users). Model-based imputation conditioned on those
          features is principled.
    MNAR  missingness depends on the UNOBSERVED value itself (high earners
          decline to state income). No imputation recovers it. The best move is
          usually to keep the missingness as a feature.

Practical consequences:
    - Always add an `is_missing` indicator for non-trivial null rates. The fact
      of missingness is frequently more predictive than the imputed value.
    - Compare the target rate for missing vs present. A large gap means MNAR-ish
      structure and possible leakage (the field may only be populated after the
      outcome).
    - Fit imputers on TRAIN ONLY and apply to val/test. A median computed over
      the full dataset is leakage.
    - Tree ensembles can handle missing natively (LightGBM/XGBoost learn a
      default direction); linear models and neural nets cannot.

Mechanics: ../../FeatureEngineering/theory/missing_data.txt

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. OUTLIERS AND ANOMALIES: DECIDE WHAT THEY MEAN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three kinds, three responses:
    Data error      impossible values -> null them out and fix the pipeline
    Rare-but-real   a genuine whale customer -> keep; winsorise or log-transform
                    if the model is sensitive, never silently delete
    The signal      in fraud/failure detection the outliers ARE the target ->
                    deleting them deletes the problem

Detection: z-score only if roughly normal; IQR fences as a robust default;
modified z-score with MAD for heavy tails; Mahalanobis or Isolation Forest for
multivariate outliers that are unremarkable in every single dimension.
See ../../FeatureEngineering/theory/outliers.txt and Ensemble_Learning/ for
Isolation Forest.

Sensitivity matters: linear regression, k-means and PCA are dominated by
extremes; tree splits and rank-based methods barely notice them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. DUPLICATES AND NOISE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Exact duplicates      often a re-delivered event or a bad join fan-out.
                          Check the join first - deduplicating the symptom hides
                          a broken pipeline.
    Near duplicates       same entity, different formatting; retweets, scraped
                          mirrors. Use fuzzy matching / MinHash / embeddings.
    Cross-split duplicates  THE dangerous one. The same record in train and test
                          gives you an inflated score that no amount of
                          cross-validation reveals. Deduplicate BEFORE splitting,
                          and split by entity (see 04_data_splitting_validation).
    Bots and automation   heavy right tail in event counts per user; strip
                          before computing behavioural aggregates.

Noise: label noise (fix the guideline, use overlap), feature noise (smoothing,
aggregation over longer windows), and quantisation artefacts. Some noise is
irreducible error - the sigma^2 term in ../../bias_variance.txt.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. UNIVARIATE ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Numeric: mean vs median gap (skew), std, IQR, min/max, skewness, kurtosis, and
always a histogram plus a quantile table (1, 5, 25, 50, 75, 95, 99). The
percentile table catches what mean/std hide: spikes at zero, truncation at a
cap, a second mode.

What the shapes tell you:
    Heavy right skew (revenue, counts, durations) -> log1p or Box-Cox for
        linear models; irrelevant for trees.
    Multimodal        -> a latent segment is hiding in the column; consider a
        segment feature or separate models.
    Spike at zero     -> zero-inflated; model as two parts (did it happen? how
        much given it happened?).
    Hard cap / floor  -> censoring. Treating a censored value as observed biases
        everything; consider survival methods.

Categorical: frequency table, cardinality, share of the top-k levels, share of
rare levels (candidates for an "other" bucket), unseen-level risk at serving.

Target: class balance (see ../../handling_imbalanced_data.txt), or for
regression the distribution and its tail. If the target is skewed, decide early
whether you predict y or log(y) - it changes the loss you are implicitly
minimising and the metric that makes sense.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. BIVARIATE AND CORRELATION ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    numeric-numeric      Pearson (linear), Spearman (monotonic, rank-based,
                         robust to outliers and skew), scatter with a smoother
    numeric-categorical  group means/medians, box plots, ANOVA F, eta-squared
    categorical-categorical  contingency table, chi-square, Cramer's V
    non-linear           mutual information, or the gain from a single-feature
                         gradient boosted stump ensemble

Correlation caveats worth saying out loud in an interview: Pearson only sees
linear structure (Anscombe's quartet), correlation is not causation, and a
feature uncorrelated with the target in isolation can still be highly useful in
interaction with another (XOR is the canonical case). This is why filter-only
feature selection is weak - see 05_feature_selection.txt.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. MULTICOLLINEARITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition: one feature is (nearly) a linear combination of others, so X^T X is
near-singular.

Detection:
    - correlation matrix > |0.8| pairs (catches pairwise only)
    - VIF_j = 1 / (1 - R_j^2), where R_j^2 comes from regressing feature j on
      all others. VIF > 5 is suspicious, > 10 is severe. VIF catches multi-way
      collinearity that a pairwise matrix misses.
    - condition number of the scaled design matrix (> 30 is a warning)

Consequences:
    - Coefficient VARIANCE explodes; signs flip between refits or CV folds.
      Coefficients become uninterpretable and unstable.
    - Predictive accuracy is usually FINE. Collinearity hurts inference, not
      prediction, as long as the collinearity structure holds at serving time.
    - Feature-importance mass gets split arbitrarily between correlated
      features, so "unimportant" is not "useless" (see 09_interpretability).

Fixes: drop one of the pair, combine them (ratio, sum, difference), PCA on the
correlated block (loses interpretability), or L2 regularisation - ridge is the
canonical answer because it shrinks correlated coefficients together and
stabilises the solution. See ../../regularization_concept.txt. Perfect
collinearity from one-hot encoding (the dummy trap) is a special case: drop one
level for linear models with an intercept.

Trees are largely immune to collinearity for prediction, but their split-based
importances are diluted by it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. LEAKAGE DETECTION DURING EDA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Red flags, in order of how often they turn out to be leakage:
    - a single feature with AUC > 0.95 on its own
    - a validation score dramatically better than any reasonable prior
    - a feature whose name contains "final", "total", "closed", "reason",
      "status", "resolved", "amount_refunded"
    - a feature whose null rate differs enormously between classes
    - an ID or timestamp with strong predictive power (rows were sorted by
      outcome; the ID encodes the collection batch)

The test: for each suspect feature ask "would this value, with this value, have
existed in the database one second before the prediction time?" If you cannot
answer with certainty, trace its lineage. Do not accept a suspiciously good
model. See also 04_data_splitting_validation.txt section on leakage taxonomy.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. WHAT TO PRODUCE AT THE END OF STAGE 3
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    - a data quality report with the automated checks and their status
    - a data dictionary: column, meaning, source, grain, null rate, availability
      at serving time
    - a shortlist of candidate features with their measured relationship to the
      target
    - a list of confirmed leakage risks and how they are being excluded
    - the split strategy implied by what you found (entity leakage? time
      structure? group structure?) - the input to stage 4
