Understanding Outliers
---------------------------------------
Outliers are extreme data points that deviate significantly from central patterns, typically arising from human error, faulty instruments, or genuine rare events.
They are categorized into global (extreme across the whole dataset), contextual (abnormal only in specific conditions), and collective (a sequence or group of anomalous points).

Impact on Machine Learning Models
---------------------------------------
Linear & Logistic Regression (Highly Sensitive): Outliers skew the ordinary least squares line, heavily inflate error metrics like RMSE, and distort classification decision boundaries.
AdaBoost (Extremely Sensitive): Because it sequentially increases the weight of misclassified points, AdaBoost disproportionately focuses on outliers, leading to severe overfitting to noise.
Deep Learning (Highly Sensitive): Outliers can trigger massive gradient updates (exploding gradients), destabilize the loss landscape, and cause the network to perfectly memorize noise due to its high capacity.
Tree-Based Models (Robust): Algorithms like Decision Trees, Random Forests, and XGBoost naturally quarantine extreme values into specific leaf nodes, preventing them from skewing the overall model structure (provided the model is not overfitted).


Detection Methods
---------------------------------------
Z-Score: Ideal for normally distributed data; it identifies outliers as data points falling beyond 3 standard deviations from the mean.
Interquartile Range (IQR): Best suited for skewed distributions; it flags points falling below Q1−1.5×IQR or above Q3+1.5×IQR.
Median Absolute Deviation (MAD): A robust method that relies on the median rather than the mean, making it highly resistant to extreme values in heavy-tailed data.
Multivariate Techniques: Mahalanobis Distance detects outliers by factoring in the correlations between multiple variables, while algorithms like Isolation Forest, Local Outlier Factor (LOF), and One-Class SVM isolate anomalies in high-dimensional spaces.


Treatment and Remediation Strategies
---------------------------------------
Trimming: Involves completely removing outlier records; it is effective for known errors but risks making the dataset too thin and losing real-world variance.
Winsorization (Capping): Replaces extreme values with a predetermined threshold (e.g., capping values at the 5th and 95th percentiles), successfully mitigating outlier impact while preserving the total sample size.
Transformations & Binning: Logarithmic or Box-Cox transformations compress extreme values inward, while discretization (binning) groups continuous variables into intervals to hide extreme spikes.
Neural Network Regularization: For deep learning, combining robust loss functions (like Huber or Transformed Trimmed Loss) with Higher-Order Variation Regularization helps the network ignore outliers while maintaining its ability to learn complex patterns.
